    How to create an invoice in 60 seconds

    1. Add your business details. Enter your name, email and address. Include your tax or VAT ID if you have one. Upload a logo once and it is remembered for future invoices.
    2. Fill in the client. Use the exact legal name of the company or person paying you, plus the email of the person who handles payments.
    3. Check the invoice number and dates. QuickInvoice suggests the next sequential number, today's date and a due date 14 days out. Adjust the due date to match the terms you agreed on.
    4. Add line items. One row per service or product with a clear description, quantity and unit price. The amount and totals calculate automatically.
    5. Set tax, discount and shipping. Enter your tax rate as a percentage, and any discount as a percentage or a fixed amount.
    6. Download the PDF. Click Download PDF, pick Save as PDF in the print dialog and send the file to your client. Click Save invoice to keep a copy in your history.

    What an invoice must include

    Requirements vary by country, but a valid, professional invoice almost always contains the following elements. Missing any of them is the most common reason invoices get bounced back by an accounts department.

    The word "Invoice" and a unique number

    Each invoice needs a unique, sequential identifier such as INV-0042. Tax authorities in most jurisdictions require sequential numbering with no gaps, and clients use the number to reference payments. Never reuse a number, even if an invoice is cancelled: issue a credit note instead.

    Issue date and due date

    The issue date establishes when the payment clock starts and which tax period the sale belongs to. The due date makes your expectations explicit. "Net 14" or "Net 30" are common, but a specific calendar date is clearer and easier to chase.

    Seller and buyer identity

    Your legal business name, address and contact details, plus the client's legal name and billing address. If you are registered for VAT, GST or sales tax, your registration number is usually mandatory. In the EU, B2B invoices typically also need the customer's VAT number.

    Itemized description of goods or services

    Each line should describe what was delivered, the quantity or hours, the unit price and the line total. Vague descriptions like "consulting" slow approval; "Website redesign – 5 pages, delivered 12 Oct" gets paid.

    Taxes, discounts and the total due

    Show the subtotal, any discount, the tax rate and tax amount as separate lines, then the grand total in a clearly labelled currency. If a sale is tax-exempt or uses reverse charge, say so on the invoice.

    Payment terms and how to pay

    State the accepted methods (bank transfer, card, PayPal), the account details, any late-payment fee, and a short thank-you. The notes field at the bottom of the invoice is the right place for this.

    Tips to get paid faster

    • Invoice immediately. Send the invoice the day the work is delivered. Every day of delay on your side becomes a day of delay on theirs.
    • Address it to the right person. Ask who processes payments before you start the project and put that email on the invoice.
    • Keep terms short and specific. Net 14 gets paid faster than Net 30, and a due date like "15 November 2026" gets paid faster than "within 14 days".
    • Offer more than one payment method. A bank transfer is cheapest for you, but a card or PayPal option removes friction for small clients.
    • Mention late fees politely. A line such as "1.5% monthly late fee applies after the due date" is standard and legal in most places.
    • Follow up on a schedule. A friendly reminder on the due date and a firmer one seven days later recovers most late invoices without damaging the relationship.
